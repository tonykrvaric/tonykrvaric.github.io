Statewide Ballot Measures
Prop 1
NO
$11.25B housing bond — bonds roughly double in cost with interest; a wasteful way to fund programs
Prop 2
NO
Tweaks Gann Limit and rainy-day fund rules but doesn't restore spending limits weakened by Props. 98 & 111 — a partial fix at best
Prop 3
NO
Makes the 2012 income tax rate hike permanent — a backdoor permanent tax increase disguised as an extension
Prop 4
NO
Forces taxpayers to fund politicians' campaign costs — government has no business financing political campaigns
Prop 5
NO
Lets a recalled officeholder run in their own recall election — undermines the entire purpose of the recall process
Prop 37
NO
$25B bond for government second-mortgage loans — housing is better served by removing building barriers, not more debt
Prop 38
NO
$8.4B bond for immune-system disease research — private, foundation, and federal funding already covers this
Prop 39
YES
Voter ID requirement — most states already require ID; prevents impersonation and ensures valid mail ballots
Prop 40
NO
Wealth tax on assets over $1B — Legislature can later lower thresholds, change rates, and expand to anyone
Prop 41
YES
Requires pre-election audits of programs funded by new statewide special taxes — accountability before the money flows
Prop 42
YES
Bars new state taxes on owning personal property and bars retroactive taxes — protects homes, savings, and retirement accounts
Prop 43
YES
Local Taxpayer Protection Act — restores the two-thirds vote requirement for local special taxes and closes the "Upland" loophole that gutted Prop. 13
Prop 44
NO
Sponsored by a labor union to expand control over community health clinics serving low-income patients
Prop 45
YES
Carves some projects out of CEQA to speed development — imperfect but a good first step toward cutting red tape
